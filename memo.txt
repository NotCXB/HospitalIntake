Trees are a great data structure for organizing doctors since they allow information to be stored in a hierarchical and organized way. For example, DoctorTree class uses nodes as individual doctors, where each doctor then has a left and right child. A tree makes it easier to navigate through this information without having to search through every doctor individually. They allows hospitals to expand their structure if they add new doctors or departments.
A Software engineers might use preorder, inorder, and postorder traversals depending on how they need to process information. For a Preorder traversal it might visit the parent before its children, making it useful for displaying a hospital's organizational structure starting from the top. Then Inorder traversal visits the left child, parent, and then right child, which can be useful when processing information in a specific sequence. Finally, Postorder traversal visits the children before the parent, making it useful when deleting a hierarchy or completing tasks that depend on lower-level employees first. In this project, we implemented all three methods using recursion.
Heaps are handy to simulate real time systems like emergency room intake where patients can be ordered by urgency. Our MinHeap class stores patients with urgency values between 1 and 10, with lower numbers meaning more priority. If a patient is inserted , the heap automatically sorts itself through heapify_up . Similarly, heapify_down is used to keep the correct order when a patient is deleted. This way the hospital can quickly identify and treat the most urgent patients as more patients come in. To summarize, this research demonstrated that trees and heaps are good ways to structure information and manage priorities.
